# Analysing human behaviour in COVID-19 lockdowns

| Date | Organisation | Topic | Type |
| :----: | :------------: | :-----: | :----: |
| 2023/Ongoing | UCL Centre for digital Public Health in Emergencies | COVID-19 and Public Health | Quantitative Research |

**Awarded "Best Research" at IEEE SeGAH 2024 (Madeira).**

How did your daily behaviour during COVID-19 lockdowns change over time? UCL dPHE published an app in June 2020 that provided a gamified journalling service, inspiring more than a thousand users to record their activities every day for their own mental wellbeing and for the purposes of our behavioural research.

In 2023, I began working on analysing this data -- at first as part of a paid summer internship, then afterward in my free time. The data is difficult to work with and all the idiosyncrasies of human behaviour make robust statistical analyses difficult, but I've been able to get quite far by focusing on the question: how did users react to the frequent changes in UK lockdown policy enforced by the government?

Initial findings indicate a mix of inertia and high-responsiveness, especially with regards to sporting and social activity. However, more statistical significance is visible when users are categorised as urban and non-urban. 

In summer 2024, I presented the first half of our research, my first-authored paper on the efficacy of the app's gamification, at IEEE SeGAH 2024 in Madeira, Portugal. While on the plane back home, I was informed I had been awarded Best Paper on account of my multifaceted approach to evidencing claims.

The second half of the research -- large sets of time series regressions on the data -- is ongoing.
