# Giving vulnerable students a voice against bureaucracy 

| Date | Organisation | Topic | Type |
| :----: | :------------: | :-----: | :----: |
| 2022/23 | The Cheese Grater (UCL) | Social Justice | Investigative Journalism |

With less fanfare than my other Cheese Grater piece, this article has nonetheless embedded itself into my head. A colleague and I interviewed another student -- older than either of us, but still a young adult -- who really laid bare his experience with UCL's inane bureacracy and how it led to heartbreaking distress. 

The interview probably lasted two hours or so. A majority of that time was an almost unbroken account, by the student, of three years of failure by the UCL administrative staff to enroll him, accomodate him in university residences, facilitate his needs, simply remember him during COVID, allow him to take a break upon family health problems, authorise his extenuating circumstances, keep their own promises, and finally hold themselves accountable when it all ended in a suicide attempt. I guess I saw a picture of what life is like if you simply get unlucky. 

I don't think the full extent of the story really came through in the article. Nonetheless, you can read it [here](https://cheesegratermagazine.org/2023/01/18/ucl-admin/).
